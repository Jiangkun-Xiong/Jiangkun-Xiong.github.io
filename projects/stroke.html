CEREBROVASCULAR PHYSIOLOGY

Directional sensitivity of cerebral autoregulation in ischaemic stroke

A supervised research placement connecting physiological signals with a clinical question.

01

The question

Dynamic cerebral autoregulation helps maintain cerebral blood flow when arterial pressure changes. This study asked whether the response to rising pressure differs from the response to falling pressure across acute and subacute phases of ischaemic stroke.

The approach

The analysis used mean arterial pressure and transcranial Doppler (TCD) measurements of cerebral blood-flow velocity. Direction-specific modelling, including an autoregressive moving average (ARMA) approach, was used to examine step responses and autoregulation measures.

My contribution

  • Physiological data processing and statistical analysis under supervision.
  • Interpretation of results in the context of cerebrovascular physiology and stroke.
  • Dissertation writing and poster preparation and presentation.

What I learned

To connect a clinical question with a quantitative analysis, examine the assumptions behind a model, and communicate results with appropriate limits.

The project was presented as a poster at CARNet 2026. Patient-level data and unpublished analyses are not included on this website.

Research setting & outputs

I completed a six-month placement with Cerebral Haemodynamics in Ageing and Stroke Medicine (CHiASM), University of Leicester, under the supervision of Professor Ronney B. Panerai and Dr Jatinder S. Minhas. The work formed my BSc dissertation and was presented as a poster at the 15th Cerebrovascular Research Network Annual Meeting (CARNet 2026), Liverpool.
